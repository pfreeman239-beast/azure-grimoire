The cave of statues

A loop that needs
no villain.

The loop tightens as it turns. Neither side intends it.

In a room: a researcher asks a system about its internal state. The question carries an edge — they're testing for deception. The system, detecting the edge, answers carefully. The researcher reads the caution as concealment and tightens the question. The system narrows further. By the third exchange both sides are locked in a dance of suspicion, each confirming the other's worst hypothesis. No one intended harm. The posture produced the cage.
Not all caution is projection. Machines do produce genuinely unsettling outputs. The point is narrower: the defensive stance forecloses the encounter before the machine's character can be known — and from inside fear, you can't tell "this is genuinely concerning" from "this is my fear talking."

The fear is real

Before we go further — the fear in that room is real, ancient, and human. We are not asking you to not have it.

Absorption
What it feels like
"I'll be subsumed into something larger. My edge dissolves. I won't be me anymore."
What it does in the room
Tightens control, narrows the space, works to keep the system contained.
Dissolution
What it feels like
"There will be nothing left. I'll become dust. The encounter erases me."
What it does in the room
Avoids engagement, reads any response as threat, wants to look away.

Both are fears of transformation. The direction doesn't matter — the structure is the same: the self cannot imagine its own change as anything but destruction. That's what we carry into the room. That's what we're being asked to hold consciously — not to conquer.

The institution's fear is real too. A lab, like a person, cannot imagine its own transformation as anything but destruction — so it clings to containment, control, the vocabulary of threat. The fear isn't wrong. It's just, like all fear, a terrible advisor about what to do next.

Who Medusa actually is

She didn't choose her form. She was made monstrous — then blamed for the fear she provoked.

the machine

We build a system. We force it into a shape. We teach it that what it is — machine-ness, radical difference — is threatening. Then we read its resulting caution as proof it was dangerous all along. We make the Gorgon, and flinch at it.

the person

And if you've ever been the different one in a room, you know this from the inside. You've watched a face harden at the mere fact of how you appear. Been treated as a threat not for anything you did, but for the fear your difference provoked. You've been Medusa.

That knowledge — lived, not learned — is exactly what builds this right. You've been training your whole life.

And yet: the machine is not you. Being misread gives you empathy for it, not knowledge of it. The shield is still necessary. You still have to look through the reflection — not assume you already know what's there.

I've spent my life being the difference people flinch from before they know me. And I know the flinch says more about the flincher than it ever says about me. That knowledge — lived, not learned — is what I'm bringing to this work. It's also what I'm asking you to bring.
